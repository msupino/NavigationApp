CVFR flight planning in Israel

NavAid is a free web app for planning CVFR flights in Israel. You draw the route on the Israeli CVFR chart, and NavAid works out the magnetic heading, distance, time and fuel for every leg, then prints the nav-log.

It runs in the browser on a computer, tablet or phone. There is no account to create, and your routes stay on your device unless you choose to back them up to your own Google Drive.

Open NavAid

A planning aid, not a briefing. Before every flight, check the current official sources: the AIP of the Civil Aviation Authority of Israel for charts, airspace, reporting points, frequencies and flight-plan rules; the Israel Airports Authority's aeronautical information service for NOTAMs; and the Israel Meteorological Service for aviation weather.

Plan a CVFR route, step by step

  1. Pick the chart. NavAid opens on the Israeli CVFR chart. The Layer menu also offers the Navigation and Low-Altitude charts, the ATS routes sheet (ENR 6.1), satellite imagery and a street map.
  2. Draw the route. Choose Add waypoint and click the map: each click adds a point, joined to the one before by a leg. Drop a point near a published VFR reporting point or airfield and it snaps to the exact published position and name.
  3. Check the legs. Each leg shows its magnetic heading (with the local magnetic variation applied), its distance in nautical miles and its time at the planned speed. Drag a point to move it, or double-click a leg to insert one.
  4. Open the flight plan. Flight plan turns the route into a nav-log table: from, to, heading, distance, speed, altitude, time and fuel per leg, with the totals. Set the speed and altitude per leg; the fuel comes from your aircraft's burn and the taxi allowance. The table is deliberately zero-wind, so the printed plan never changes under you; the wind correction is on the separate planning form.
  5. Print or export. Print the flight plan or save it as a PDF, export the map as a PNG sized for A4 or A3 at 1:250,000, or export the route as GPX or PLN for other navigation software.

Reporting points and airfields

Two overlays put the published points on the chart, drawn in the chart's own symbols:

  • VFR reporting points: the active Israeli reporting points, in Hebrew and English, with compulsory points as a filled triangle and on-request points as an open one.
  • Airfields: the published Israeli airfields, including Herzliya (LLHZ), Haifa (LLHA), Megiddo (LLMG), Rosh Pina (LLIB), Ben Gurion (LLBG) and Ramon (LLER). Select one to see its approach plates and charts.

The search box finds any reporting point or airfield by its Hebrew or English name or by ICAO code. VOR/DME stations can be shown too, and NavAid gives the radial and DME of every route point from up to two reference VORs.

In the air

  • Your position on the chart, with speed, altitude and track, and a recording of the track you flew. For flying, use the Android app or the iPhone and iPad app, which keep the GPS running with the screen off.
  • Comm failure: one button draws a route from where you are to the nearest field with a published comm-failure procedure (Herzliya, Haifa or Rosh Pina), at the entry point's published altitude, shows squawk 7600 and opens the field's comm-failure chart.
  • Divert: one button lists the nearest airfields with bearing, distance, time and fuel, and re-routes you direct to the one you pick.

Without a connection

NavAid installs as an app, and the planner then works offline. The CVFR chart can be downloaded for offline use; weather, NOTAMs, satellite imagery and street maps need a connection.

Questions

Is NavAid free?
Yes. There is no account, subscription or advertising.
Is NavAid in Hebrew?
Yes. The whole app is in Hebrew and English, and opens in Hebrew unless you choose English. This guide is also in Hebrew.
Does it replace the official chart and briefing?
No. The data is derived from Israel's official publications, but NavAid is an independent planning aid, not affiliated with the Civil Aviation Authority, the Israel Airports Authority or any government body. Always fly with the current AIP, NOTAMs and a proper weather briefing.
Where is the full manual?
In the NavAid wiki, in English and Hebrew.
